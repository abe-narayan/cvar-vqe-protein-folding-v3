# S28 LANE B -- THE FIRST NON-DIAGONAL HAMILTONIAN: ENERGY PLUS HOPPING ON THE POOL GRAPH

You are lane B. Read `s27/S28_CONTRACT.md` first.

## Why this, in the record's own numbers
Every Hamiltonian the project has run is diagonal in the candidate basis, H = diag(E), and the
S24/S25 set-equality theorem says the CVaR tail of any such H is a subset of the classical
energy prefix (2,592 adversarial cells, 0 violations; S27 T9: 80 energies x 126 targets x 2
seeds, 0 violations). A diagonal H has nothing for a circuit to do that a sort does not. The
CVaR-VQE can only matter if H has off-diagonal structure whose ground state is not a basis state.

The structure the pool offers is its similarity graph: A_ij = exp(-d_ij^2 / 2 sigma^2) on the
pairwise CA-RMSD of the 500 windows (native-free; `core.backend("numerics").kabsch_rmsd_batch`
gives a row per call; 500 x 500 per target is cheap). The Hamiltonian H = diag(E) - J A
(E = zrank of the shipped score, J >= 0) is the tight-binding Hamiltonian of a particle on the
pool graph with on-site energies E: its ground state delocalises over a cluster of low-energy,
mutually similar candidates. This is a real quantum object (real symmetric, off-diagonal), and
the question is whether its ground-state weights, consumed by the deployed readout, emit a better
structure than the classical prefix, and whether the circuit can find them.

## Pre-register (`s27/PREREG_S28_B.md`) before any endpoint number
- The objective keeps the spine: F(theta) = CVaR_alpha(E; p) - T H(p) - J <psi|A|psi>, with the
  hopping term evaluated on the statevector (real amplitudes; `StatevectorCircuit`), its
  parameter-shift gradient added to `core.quantum.free_energy`'s. S25 settings (n = 9 over the
  500 + 12 padding states with A = 0 on padding, alpha 0.18, T 0.5, 80 Adam iterations, seed 0
  and 1). J in {0, 0.1, 0.3, 1.0} x the scale of E's gaps (state the normalisation); sigma set
  native-free at the median pairwise RMSD of the pool.
- Readouts, each a separate arm: (i) the CVaR tail of the measured p (the deployed readout,
  uniform average, then the built chain); (ii) the p-weighted average (S23 L8 measured it worse
  at J = 0; at J > 0 it is a new object); (iii) the exact ground state of H by `numpy.linalg.eigh`
  read as p = v^2 (the classical spectral counterpart: if (i)/(ii) at the VQE optimum do no
  better than (iii), the circuit added nothing beyond an eigensolver).
- Controls: J = 0 (reproduces S25's 3.0580 VQE arm on the point cloud); A rank-permuted (the
  graph destroyed, the amplitude kept); A replaced by a random geometric graph with the same
  degree distribution; the random-75 null.
- Falsifiers. "Hopping helps": some J arm beats J = 0 on the built chain beyond its MDE with the
  fold CI excluding zero on 5/5 folds, on both seeds, and beats its permuted-A control. "The
  circuit matters": (i) or (ii) at the VQE optimum beats (iii) beyond MDE (the variational state
  finds a better weighting than the exact ground state, which would be a regularisation effect;
  prior: the eigensolver wins or ties). Registered prior for "hopping helps": WORSE, because
  delocalising over a similar cluster is the consistency mechanism S27 section 6 measured as
  harmful (+0.29 A for DIS+CONS); the value of the experiment is that it is the first non-diagonal
  H and it settles whether a quantum-native coupling changes the S27 verdict.
- The quantum-side measurement that stands on its own: the gradient variance of F at n = 4..9
  (depth 3, theta ~ N(0, 0.6^2), exact parameter-shift, `s25/q_plateau.py :: measure` extended
  with the hopping term) as a function of J, and the set-equality gate's behaviour (at J > 0 the
  theorem no longer applies: report how far the tail departs from the energy prefix, which is
  the first measured departure in the project). Never call any slope a barren plateau or its
  absence.
- Diagnostics: participation ratio of the ground state, its overlap with the DIS top-75, the
  cluster it selects (mean pairwise RMSD of the tail), FAIL18 vs the rest, NaN-poison, cost.
