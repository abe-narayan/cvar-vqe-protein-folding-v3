# REPORT S26, one-page summary

Full report: `docs/REPORT_S26.md` (Parts I to X, Appendices A to D). Every number below carries its artefact path; the full list is Appendix B of the report.

## What the project is

A pipeline that predicts the backbone (Cα) shape of short peptides, 9 to 16 residues, from sequence. It retrieves 500 real fragments of the same length by BLOSUM62 similarity, scores each against a learned 17-bin distance prior (a distogram trained on 787 peptides plus 6,003 protein fragments, leave-fold-out), keeps the 75 best, averages them in the medoid's frame, and projects the average onto a chain with ideal 3.8 Å spacing and Ramachandran-penalised torsions (`core/pipeline.py`; `ARCHITECTURE.md`, frozen at commit `a15406c`). A 7-qubit, 21-parameter CVaR-VQE (RY/CNOT ansatz, exact statevector, Adam on exact parameter-shift gradients, objective CVaR_α − T·H over a diagonal rank-ladder Hamiltonian) can replace the top-75 cut; it ships off. Everything is measured on a 126-target cluster-disjoint instrument with paired statistics, a per-comparison minimum detectable effect (2.8016 × SE), fold-clustered confidence intervals, pre-registered falsifiers from Sprint 16 on, and one sealed 60-target benchmark spent once.

## The headline

Mean Cα RMSD **3.2148 Å** over 126 targets on the built-chain basis (`bench_results/compare_tuning126.json`, `science.rmsd_arm`; the results-lab rebuild prints 3.2126, `results/summary/leaderboard.csv`); median 2.9661; best 0.182 Å (1S9Z); worst 8.234 Å (2MQ2); 28.6% of targets under 2 Å, 50.8% under 3 Å (`results/summary/leaderboard.csv`). The point-cloud average, 3.0483 Å, is an intermediate, not a structure (`core/bench.py` line 682, "raw average (illegal)"). On the sealed benchmark the tuned pipeline is +0.0103 Å [−0.1596, +0.1803] against the baseline, 31W/29L: the −0.250 Å tuning gain did not transfer (`s9/final_report.json` via `verify/headline_audit.json`). Reproduction is bit-identical between 1 and 8 workers (`compare_tuning126.json`) and on a fresh re-run (`s26/results/e_reproduce.json`); 370 tests, 357 pass, 13 skip (`s26/results/test_run.json`).

## The three most important findings

1. **The distance prior is the only steep lever.** Moving the prior toward the truth is worth −2.1496 Å per unit at the origin; a perfect prior through the same pipeline gives 2.2261 Å (ORACLE, uses the native) (`s24/results/priorladder.json`; `s24/LEDGER.md` L13). Every downstream lever is flat: selection inside the pool is closed at four levels (a perfect ranker in the top-25 still returns 2.609 Å, ORACLE; `s17/results/inband.json`), the pool's error is 68% common-mode so averaging cannot remove it (`s23/LEDGER.md` L9), and both physics energies are measurably worse than a random subset as rankers (Legacy +0.3302, AMBER +0.4554 Å; `s25/results/phys_suite.json`).

2. **The quantum component is exact and contributes nothing measurable.** Verified: statevector 5.551e-17, parameter-shift gradient cosine 1.000000000 (rel. err. 4.6e-10), MPS twin 3.331e-16, and a proved theorem that the CVaR tail is a subset of the classical ranking's prefix, 0 violations on 2,592 cells (`s25/results/q_verify.json`). Contribution: −0.1405 Å against the plain argmin at 0.68× MDE, not a result; the previously quoted "+0.113 Å" was measured at a temperature that does not ship and was a marginal, not a paired statistic; it is withdrawn (`s25/results/q_alpha.json`; `s25/LEDGER.md` L3, L5). The Hamiltonian is the same rank ladder on every target to 1.18% of its range, so the circuit trains toward one of two states regardless of target (`s25/results/q_gibbs.json`; L17).

3. **The trainability chain is measured end to end, with its scope.** Chain geometry fixes which residues a distance term depends on (exact, agreement 1.0000; `s13/results/qarch_locality_geom.json`); that fixes the energy's Pauli spectrum (Legacy mean weight 2.236, conditioned AMBER 3.015, 79/79 cells; raw AMBER is a delta spike; `s13/results/geo_pauli.json`, `walsh_xval.json`); the spectrum times the circuit's gradient kernel predicts measured gradient variance to 0.4% (`s13/results/walsh_predict.json`). At depth 3, n = 4 to 13, the deployed objective's gradient variance decays at −0.24 to −0.31 log₂ per qubit (`s25/results/q_plateau.json`); the ansatz's dynamical Lie algebra is the full so(2ⁿ) from depth 2 (`s26/results/q_dla.json`), so this is a shallow-regime statement, not "no barren plateau"; and an ADAPT-grown circuit on this Hamiltonian at α = 1 is a product circuit whose large gradient is the trivial regime, not trainability (`s26/results/q_var.json`).

## The three most important open questions

1. **Can a better distance prior be learned from inputs this machine can compute?** The S26 ladder (`s26/PROPOSAL_C.md`, rungs C2 to C5) is the first attempt; ESMFold does not run here (`s26/results/b1_feasibility.json`). Settled by a rung beating the shipped prior on the fold-clustered CI.
2. **Where does the target-specific third of the pool's coherent error come from, and is any native-free proxy strong enough to act on it?** Two thirds is generic (`s19/LEDGER.md` L11); the best native-free in-band proxies reach 0.24 to 0.37 against the 0.638 that 2 Å needs (memory `in-band-ordering-is-per-target`).
3. **What does an adaptive ansatz do on a Hamiltonian that is not diagonal, and does the spectrum-times-kernel prediction extend to it?** On the diagonal Hamiltonian the endpoint is bounded by the set-equality theorem and the ADAPT endpoint (A1) is pending, expected null (`s26/PREREG_A1.md`). This is the question to put to the ADAPT-VQE group.
