## (a) Learning to rank within groups

### The construction

RankNet (Burges et al., ICML 2005) models the probability that item i outranks item j within the
same query as a logistic function of the SCORE DIFFERENCE, sigma(s_i - s_j), and minimises
cross-entropy against the known order. LambdaRank replaces the gradient with one weighted by the
change in the evaluation metric obtained by swapping the pair, so pairs whose swap matters most
dominate training. Listwise methods (ListNet, ListMLE) replace pairs with a Plackett-Luce
likelihood over the whole permutation.

### The property that matters here, and it is a one-line proof

Suppose a scorer decomposes as s(x) = f(x) + g(group) -- a per-group additive confound. Then
within a group, s_i - s_j = f(x_i) - f(x_j): the confound CANCELS EXACTLY. Every pairwise
within-group objective is therefore blind to any quantity that is constant across the group,
which is the same algebra as a fixed-effects regression, and the same algebra as CONDITIONAL
LOGISTIC REGRESSION on matched case-control sets in epidemiology. Lane D's matched-realism band
plus a pairwise objective is, formally, a matched case-control design with conditional logistic
regression as its estimator. That is the correct statistical name for what the sprint is about to
do, and it is worth using, because the matched-design literature carries the right warnings
(see (d) and topic 6's range-restriction note).

### Does LTR address the confound case? No -- and the distinction is not cosmetic

In LTR the group is a QUERY: a legitimate context that the system is asked to serve, where
cross-query score comparability is simply not part of the task. Here the group is a BAND imposed
to remove a nuisance axis. The algebra is identical; the inferential claim is not. In LTR a model
with good within-query order and no cross-query calibration is a SUCCESS. Here a model with good
within-band order and no cross-band calibration may still be useless, because the deployed
operator must eventually choose among candidates that the band construction threw away. I found
nothing in the LTR literature that treats the grouping variable as a nuisance to be removed;
that framing lives in matched-design statistics and in causal inference (backdoor adjustment),
not in LTR.

### Sample efficiency, and a caution the project needs for its power calculation

Pairwise training on n items per group generates O(n^2) pairs, but those pairs are NOT
independent: they are generated by n underlying items, and by far fewer underlying targets. The
effective sample size for a cross-target claim is the number of TARGETS (126 here, or fewer in a
band), not the number of pairs. The project already enforces fold-clustered CIs for this reason,
and Hamelryck et al.'s non-independence finding (topic 6) is the structural version of the same
warning. Any in-band pairwise result must be powered on targets, never on pairs.

### The one importable item

LambdaRank's metric weighting. Our "metric change on swapping a pair" is the realised difference
in built-chain CA-RMSD, so the natural transfer is to weight each within-band pair by |Delta RMSD|
rather than treating a 0.05 A inversion and a 1.0 A inversion equally. The project's existing
pairwise learner (S28-L48) used unweighted sign pairs. This is a genuine, cheap, unexploited
refinement -- but it is a LOSS refinement, not an information change, so by contract rule 14 it
does not qualify as an import on its own. NOTED, not KEPT.

## (b) Pairwise and contrastive training on structures

### Jing X, Dong Q et al. "Sorting protein decoys by machine-learning-to-rank." (PMC4987638)

Construction. SVMrank with a linear kernel; "training instances are pairs of decoys from the same
proteins" -- within-target pairs, exactly the design in question. Features: knowledge-based
potentials (DFIRE, DOPE, GOAP, RWplus, DIH, DFIRE-SCM, ...) plus outputs of assessment programs
(Frst, ProQ, RFMQA, SIFT, SELECTpro).

What the ranking objective bought, on 3DRobot: classification 0.30 wmPMCC / 0.68 AUC; regression
(SVMlight) 0.88 / 0.94; **pairwise ranking 0.95 / 0.95**. The authors conclude the LTR
formulation "is more appropriate for evaluating protein model quality".

The assumption that breaks the transfer. 3DRobot spans **RMSD 0 to 12 A**. That is the widest
possible between-band comparison, and the gain from regression (0.88) to ranking (0.95) is
measured there. The paper trains on CASP10 and tests on CASP11 and does NOT analyse whether
within-target pairwise training transfers across targets -- which is precisely the quantity S14
measured here and found to collapse (0.986 within, 0.600 across).

### Hurtado, Uziela & Elofsson, ProQ4 (arXiv:1804.06281) -- already in L_1

The Siamese rank-loss fine-tuning is the QA literature's own pairwise variant. Its reported
per-model correlation is 0.56 against ProQ3D's 0.61, i.e. the rank loss did not dominate the
absolute-label method on that axis; its advantage is in "first rank loss" (the average difference
between the best and the top-ranked model), which is the model-selection metric.

### "Protein quality assessment with a loss function designed for high-quality decoys" (Frontiers in Bioinformatics, 2023; PMC10616882)

Construction. A quality-dependent epsilon-insensitive loss, L(y, y') = max(0, |y - y'| - eps(y)),
where the tolerance narrows as quality rises (eps = 0.45 for scores 0-0.1; eps = 0.01 for scores
> 0.8) -- the explicit rationale being that "the model should not try too hard to accurately fit
poor-quality decoys where we do not need good accuracy anyhow". Overall correlation improves
0.75 (L1 only) to 0.90 in ablation.

The gap in it, which matters for us: the paper reports no disaggregated performance on the
high-quality subset it designed the loss for. So even the paper explicitly targeting the
high-quality regime does not report in-band skill separately. This independently corroborates
topic 6's "nobody has done this".

### The information test, applied

What would a pairwise/contrastive model see that our 31 scorers do not? **Nothing.** It consumes
the same features with a different loss. And the project has already run the experiment: S28-L48's
nested pairwise-logistic combination of all 31 scorers reached 0.960 held-out sign accuracy
against a per-target sign-flip null of 0.502 -- and the SAME rule on the RAND_SIGNED control
reached 0.952, head-to-head 0.492. It learned "is this a real protein trace", which is the realism
axis, which is precisely what a matched-realism band removes.
REJECTED as an import. KEPT as a prediction: if D bands on realism and then trains the same
pairwise learner, the 0.960 should collapse toward chance, because the band removes the axis that
produced it. That collapse is a PREDICTION D can check cheaply, and it would validate the band
construction itself before anything is concluded from a null.

## (c) The free-energy class, and a problem with it nobody here has stated

### What the cheap surrogates are, and how they fail

Quasi-harmonic entropy (Karplus & Kushick 1981): diagonalise the covariance matrix of the
coordinates sampled from an ensemble and sum the entropies of independent harmonic oscillators.
Numata, Wan & Knapp (2007, "Conformational entropy of biomolecules: beyond the quasi-harmonic
approximation") state the key property: the largest classical eigenvalues "tend to be more
anharmonic and show statistical dependence beyond correlation", and a k-nearest-neighbour
information-theoretic correction "calculates a TIGHTER UPPER LIMIT to entropy than the
quasi-harmonic approximation". So S_qh is an **upper bound** on the true entropy in the converged
limit, and F_qh = <E> - T S_qh is correspondingly an **under**estimate of the free energy. The
literature also reports that quasi-harmonic estimates have "severe convergence problems" and
assume a single energy minimum, "an approximation too crude for flexible structures". Normal-mode
entropy is the other standard route and is described as "very computationally expensive", usually
truncated.

### The two biases fight at short sampling -- a reconciliation for S8's stage

`docs/FINDINGS.md` flags S8's own S_qh as "noisy and downward-biased" at 4 ps / 100 snapshots
over up to 42 CA degrees of freedom. That is not in conflict with the literature's "upper bound":
the quasi-harmonic FUNCTIONAL FORM is an upper bound once converged, while FINITE SAMPLING
underestimates the covariance (the basin has not been explored), pushing S_qh down. At 4 ps the
finite-sample downward bias dominates. The two biases have opposite signs and different
per-candidate magnitudes, so neither cancels in a within-target ranking. This is my reading of
the two sources together, labelled as such, and it is a reason to distrust a 4 ps F_qh RANKING
specifically -- the noise is not merely additive.

### The problem nobody here has stated, and it is the important part

A free energy F = E - T S ranks differently from E only if S VARIES across candidates in a way
not collinear with E. The free-energy class was wanted (from L_1) because the converged-MD
selector is a free energy and single-point energies are measured anti here. But what does S
actually track for a 9-16-mer? Basin width -- the volume of configuration space accessible around
the candidate. Compact, well-packed conformations have narrower basins; extended ones have wider
basins. So the entropy term is plausibly a COMPACTNESS-LIKE statistic, on the same axis as
Legacy (a compactness model, top-75 Rg -0.758 A), RG_LAW, RG_UNIV, EXVOL and CAGEO.

If that is right, the free-energy class is not orthogonal to the realism axis at all -- it is
another reading of it, and both of the coordinator's live ideas (the matched-realism band and the
free-energy stage) would be acting on the SAME axis, in opposite directions. This is a
hypothesis, not a measurement, and it is cheap to test before spending the AMBER budget:
correlate S8's `width` / `S_msf` channels (already computed for the one completed target, and
cheap for a handful more) against Rg and against the existing compactness channels. If the
correlation is high, the free-energy route is not the orthogonal information source L_1 hoped
for, and the queued experiment should be re-scoped before it runs.

### The caveat the coordinator asked me to restate, restated

Any free-energy arm would need the ENTROPY term to rescue a channel (AMBER single-point) measured
**+0.455 A worse than a random subset** as a ranker, 5/5 folds (S25 L16). That is a strong claim
for a term estimated from 4 ps of Langevin dynamics with two opposed biases. Combined with the
compactness suspicion above, my recommendation is that the free-energy stage should NOT be run as
an accuracy arm first. It should be run as a three-target diagnostic answering one question: is
the entropy term orthogonal to compactness? If it is not, the class is closed cheaply. If it is,
that orthogonality is itself the interesting result and justifies the full cost.

## (d) The honest prior -- does the record already answer topic 7?

**Yes, and twice over. And there is a third thing, which is new, and which the coordinator should
see before lane D fixes its band.**

**The record's two answers.** S14 (`in-band-ordering-is-per-target`) measured a learned in-band
ranker directly: in-sample 0.986, same-target-held-out **0.986** with an overfitting gap of
-0.0005 [-0.0011, +0.0000], and cross-target **0.600** [0.549, 0.655] -- a transfer gap of -0.3859,
**770x** the overfitting gap, against a requirement of **0.638** for 2.0 A through a top-100
operator. Capacity is not the constraint: "a LINEAR 4,125-parameter pair potential already
saturates the within-target problem at 0.986, so nonlinearity, capacity, equivariance, graph or
geometric architectures cannot improve on it and none of them touches transfer, where the entire
loss sits." The learning curve DECLINES with more targets (m=1 at -0.348 beats m=12 at -0.162),
because averaging across targets cancels a per-target axis whose SIGN flips. S12
(`in-band-signal-limited-not-sample-limited`) measured the last untested architecture class, a
set-transformer over the full signed deviation map: learning curve 3.043 / 3.049 / 3.046 / 3.036
/ 3.026 at n = 8/16/32/64/75 -- **flat** -- while the identical harness with a LEAKED label is
loud at n = 8 (2.534). Signal-limited, not sample-limited.

So the honest prior for a trained in-band ranker at this length is: **it will train beautifully
within a target and transfer at approximately 0.600 against a 0.638 requirement, and no change of
loss (pairwise, listwise, lambda-weighted, contrastive) or architecture will move that, because
the measured bottleneck is the per-target SIGN of the axis, which inference cannot observe.** The
literature in (a) and (b) offers losses and architectures. The record says the constraint is
neither.

**The third thing, and it is a warning about D's experiment design.** S14's flip diagnostic found
that per-target skill correlates **+0.909 with the native's z-scored RADIUS OF GYRATION** and
**+0.951 with rho(contacts, RMSD)**. The in-band ordering axis IS compactness. Now consider the
matched-realism band: every realism statistic available in the S27 library is compactness-like --
Legacy is explicitly a compactness model, and RG_LAW, RG_UNIV, EXVOL, CAGEO, CONTACT and ENV are
all functions of packing. **If D bands on a compactness-like realism statistic, the band removes
the exact axis that carries whatever in-band skill exists.** By topic 6's partial-correlation
bound, rho_SY.R would then be crushed toward zero BY CONSTRUCTION OF THE BAND, and the resulting
null would be self-fulfilling: uninterpretable as evidence about in-band signal, because the band
was built on the signal.

The fix is cheap and must happen BEFORE the run: D should measure rho(R, Rg) for the chosen band
statistic R and report it beside the result. Three cases. (i) R is strongly compactness-loaded:
the experiment is self-defeating as designed and the band statistic must be changed or
length-residualised. (ii) R is weakly compactness-loaded: the experiment is valid, and the
measured rho(R, Rg) is exactly the number needed to interpret the effect size. (iii) R is
orthogonal to compactness: the experiment is clean, and it is then genuinely testing for a SECOND
in-band axis beyond the one S14 found -- which is the most interesting version of the experiment
and should be stated as the hypothesis. Note also the constructive corollary: S14 already
measured native-free compactness proxies at residualised r = 0.244 (pool mean Rg), 0.313
(distogram-predicted Rg) and 0.365 (incumbent emitted Rg) against the ORACLE's 0.909, all three
CIs excluding zero. Those proxies are the closest thing the project has to an in-band conditioning
signal, and they are weak -- "roughly 61% sign accuracy, against a project where fusion gain goes
as the SQUARE of the weaker channel's skill".

**Conclusion for topic 7.** If D's in-band result is null, the follow-up should NOT be a better
in-band ranker -- that is closed by S12 and S14 with positive measurements rather than failures to
find. The follow-up should be the per-target SIGN question, which is the one direction S14 left
open and named: "the only direction with leverage is anything that supplies the PER-TARGET SIGN of
the in-band axis at inference: a conditioning signal, not a better objective."
