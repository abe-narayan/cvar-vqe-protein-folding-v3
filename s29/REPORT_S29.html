The missing cosine

We spent a sprint looking for the path below 2.5 Å. We found instead that the gap is a single measurable quantity — and measured it. Every operator this project can build moves the answer sideways.

One geometric fact, seen from four sides

Why every operator fails, and why they all fail the same way

The production answer is a point in coordinate space. Everything the project can do to it without seeing the native — re-rank, re-weight, re-average, shrink, project — moves it. Write that move as a displacement d, and the direction to the native as u. Then, exactly:

RMSD_new = RMSD_prod × √(1 − ρ²)        ρ = cos(d, u)

This is not a model. It is Pythagoras with the optimal step substituted in, and it means the entire value of any operator is one number. Reaching 3.00 Å needs ρ = 0.358. Reaching 2.50 Å needs 0.628. A random shape field scores 0.1398.

Stated at three levels: ≥ 3.210 Å for every field this project has built; ≥ 3.181 Å for a random-strength field handed a perfect sign; ≥ 2.98 Å for the best structured field handed a perfect per-target oracle sign. Even the last of those — which no method could achieve — misses the interim target.

The motion is real, but lateral — and we photographed it

The most vivid demonstration arrived as a by-product. One lane’s readout emits a cloud that moves 0.22 to 1.45 Å away from production while its RMSD changes by under 0.1 Å. A large displacement that barely changes the distance to the native is a near-zero cosine, visible without computing one. The operators are not weak. They are sideways.

Why no operator finds the direction

The missing quantity is not hard to estimate. It is not estimable.

To move toward the native you must know, per target, which way. That per-target sign is an incidental parameter in the sense of Neyman & Scott (1948): a nuisance parameter whose count grows with the sample, so it cannot be estimated from other targets’ answers — not “is difficult to”, but cannot, as a matter of statistical theory. The standard remedy eliminates it rather than estimating it.

Then it was measured four independent times, and the agreement is the strongest evidence here.

The ladder, on the metric that counts

And a correction we made to our own headline

A correction, in the open

For most of this sprint the architectural ceiling was quoted as 2.7605 Å. That is a point-cloud number, and the endpoint is the built chain. The error was the coordinator’s, not the lane’s: the underlying entry was a consistent cloud-to-cloud comparison; promoting it into a headline without its basis label was not. On the endpoint the figure is 2.9027 Å, and the conclusion gets stronger, not weaker. Eighteen claims were withdrawn during this sprint; eleven were the coordinator’s own — caught by a lane, by recomputing a number from its artefact, or by simply re-running the thing. The last of them was written into this report and superseded by the lane that owned the measurement.

Two constraints, at two levels

Conflating them is easy, so they are kept apart

For anything native-free, the binding constraint is the cosine. The pool is not the limit (its hull expresses 1.12 Å), the circuit is not, the optimiser is not, and even a free readout over the top-75 is not. What is missing is a vector with ρ > 0.14.

For the deployed architecture with an oracle, the binding constraint is the readout. Holding the candidate set fixed at the top-128 the quantum stage actually sees:

how many to average (prefix length m)2.9027−0.30793.14× MDE

The same information budget, 3.5× the payoff, differing only in what it is spent on. The architecture spends its information on the wrong question.

So the obvious follow-up: how many of those 7 bits does the shipped score actually supply? We measured it directly — the rank, under the deployed score, of the genuinely best member of its own fixed top-128.

The answer, and the mean flatters it

The deployed score delivers 1.442 of 7 bits. A uniform random ranking delivers 1.405. The difference is 0.10× MDE.

And the median is worse than that: the median paired difference is −0.575 bits, the score is below the random baseline on 82 of 126 targets, and the median rank of the best member is 72 of 128 against a chance median of 64.5. The positive mean rides on a handful of targets where the best member happens to land near the top. The shipped score is at chance for locating the best member of its own top-128 — and on the typical target, slightly worse than chance.

Nor is the gap shaped like the quantity we most suspected: even oracle knowledge of the radial profile supplies only 3.20 of the 7 bits.

The caveat that makes this honest

That 0.7592 Å prices a readout switch plus the bits — not a ranking improvement fed to the operator that ships. The terminal operator consumes the set mean: d_out = 1.16·mean + 0.04·best (R² = 0.89). A perfect rank-1 signal is worth about −1.74 Å through argmin and about −0.03 Å through the shipped 75-member average. A lane caught the coordinator stating this wrongly, and the correction is in the record beside the claim.

So the two levels compose, and that is the practical conclusion: a better channel is necessary but not sufficient, because the operator that would have to spend it is an average, and an average cannot spend a ranking.

Does lower cost mean lower RMSD?

The question this project most needed answered — tested directly

The setup was the strongest available. One lane’s meter had identified a cost function that is the first in this project’s record not to anti-order the near-native ladder. If better cost ordering ever converts into better structures, this is the cost that should show it. Everything but the functional was held at production.

LOG − PRODUCTION      +0.0822 Å   0.55× MDE   NOT MEASURED
LOG − random swap     +0.0202 Å   0.14× MDE   NOT MEASURED

The better cost function’s entire endpoint effect is indistinguishable from exchanging 20.6 of its 75 members at random. And it emits the same answer: the cosine between its error vector and production’s is 0.924, against a random reference of 0.177.

The information is real — destroying the correspondence it consumes costs +0.7295 Å at 2.29× MDE. It is simply not information production lacks. A better cost moves the answer along a displacement whose cosine is ≈ 0.04, through a readout that can spend at most 0.04 of any ranking, toward a per-target sign that is not estimable. Four independent reasons, one outcome.

What the quantum stage contributed

A real result, and an honest price

CVaR is defined on sorted samples for diagonal Hamiltonians, so the deployed tail is provably a prefix of the energy order. Putting the objective on the tail’s own coordinate average breaks that. Exhaustively, over all 124,750 pairs of each of the 126 pools:

measurementresult
f-optimal pair is not the energy prefix114 / 126  (90.5%)
f-optimal 5-member subset is not a prefix124 / 126  (98.4%)
the per-state sort finds the optimum12 / 126  (9.5%)

The optimum is reachable neither by sorting E nor by sorting f. This is the first measurement in the project’s record of which set as a genuine optimisation variable rather than the read-out of a sort.

And it buys nothing measurable. The aggregate arm is +0.2451 Å worse — but two-thirds of that is simply using 5 members instead of 75, a ladder priced three times before. The part attributable to the hypothesis itself is +0.0804 at 0.73× MDE: not measured. The lane reported it that way rather than banking the aggregate as a refutation.

One distinction we hold to, because it would be easy to overclaim. That is a statement about a free subset search. The deployed circuit's tail is still the energy prefix by construction at every setting — it never escaped the theorem. The lane found a column in its own output that briefly suggested otherwise, identified it as a tie-convention artefact, and asked that the stronger claim not be made. The non-prefix optimum exists and is reachable by search; the circuit running that objective does not reach it.

The mechanism is alive, and irrelevant

Running a VQE on that objective refutes the arm exactly as pre-registered — no setting beats production or its own zero setting, on either seed. What makes it more than another null is the pair of facts underneath:

  • The objective moves the set hard. Overlap with the shipped top-75 falls from 0.93 to 0.52, and the realised tail from 74 members to 39. This is not an arm that failed to do anything.
  • And the emitted structure does not move. The readout is flat at every setting — |effect| ≤ 0.046 Å, every cell not measured.

Half the set membership changes and the answer stays put. That is the terminal-operator law seen from the quantum side.

The matching control closes it. The deployed stage's only remaining channel to the answer is its per-target choice of tail size. Measured against a fixed size with no circuit in it, on the built chain, every comparison is inside its own MDE — and the trained circuit is nominally the worst of the cluster:

The projection stage is not a fixed tax

What stayed open

The result that makes this report less complete, reported first among the unresolved

A falsifier that went against both people who registered a prior

The argument for closing the last exit was that the project’s positive in-band signals might all be compactness proxies in disguise. The coordinator expected confirmation in writing; the lane registered a prior of 3-to-1 the same way. Both were wrong.

The loadings are exactly as predicted — a compactness channel scores +0.956 against member radius of gyration. But compactness loading does not track in-band skill: the correlation between them is +0.083 against a registered bar of 0.40, partialling out size removes 9% of the top channels’ skill, and the most size-loaded channels carry the least skill. Nine of 31 channels keep skill after removing both monotone and V-shaped size dependence, against ~1.6 expected by chance.

The survivor that matters is a backbone-torsion term: local conformation, a function of the structure and not of the distance prediction — therefore outside the class the theorem bounds. The route is open in the precise sense that the argument for closing it failed, not that it is shown to work.

What would actually settle it

The next question is not “which operator”

Everything closed here was closed as a consumer of existing information. The next question is where new information comes from — and the answer has to clear two bars at once, because a channel without a readout that can spend it is worth 0.04 of itself.

How this was checked

Two verification passes, run against our own record

Every artefact path was tested for existence — 205 of the 217 cited in the ledger resolve. This check exists because the sprint discovered that a module described in the project’s findings file as “committed and resumable”, with its tests and four output files named, has never existed on disk or in git history. An artefact path in prose is a claim, not a citation.

The twelve that do not resolve are itemised rather than rounded away: six are extraction artefacts, two a rename recorded in git history, one an older sprint’s, two were this audit’s own tool cited by its scratchpad name — an entry about unresolvable citations containing one — and one is a genuine dangling citation to a file that never existed. That is the fourth such path found this sprint.

Every headline number was recomputed from its artefact, not copied from the entry reporting it: 58 of 58 match. That pass caught an error several re-readings of the prose had not — a field count of 39 quoted against a file containing 21.

Statistical discipline throughout: MDE computed per comparison rather than per instrument (the project’s old single constant is wrong by up to 84× in both directions); fold-clustered confidence intervals on 5 pinned folds; concentration scored against a uniform-effect null rather than a raw drop-top threshold; power and Type-M reported beside every effect. Below 0.7× MDE is not a result — a rule applied to this sprint’s own positives without exception, disqualifying four of them.
